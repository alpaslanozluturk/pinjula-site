Last updated: 11 October 2026

A French version of this page follows. For residents of Québec, the French version is the one you accept unless, after receiving it, you expressly choose the English version.

Before you start

Pinjula works without an account. You have one only if you signed in — with Google or Apple — to buy Premium or to use an AI feature. If you never signed in, there is no account to delete: uninstalling the app deletes everything it stores on your phone.

Deleting your account does not cancel a Premium subscription. The App Store or Google Play bills it, and keeps billing it until you cancel it there. Cancel first: on the App Store at https://apps.apple.com/account/subscriptions, on Google Play in Play Store → Profile → Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions. After you delete your account we can no longer email you the renewal reminders and notices we send subscribers; your store sends its own.

Delete your account in the app

  1. Open the app and go to Settings → Account (or Settings → Privacy & security).
  2. Tap Delete account. The app tells you that billing continues through the store until you cancel and that our subscription emails stop, links to where you can cancel, and shows what will be deleted. Tap Continue.
  3. Type the word the app shows you (DELETE in English) and tap Delete my account.

The app deletes its data on the phone, signs you out and tries to disconnect itself from Google's sign-in library, and a message confirms that your account and the app's data on this phone have been deleted.

Ask us to delete it without the app

If you no longer have the app, or can't open it, email support@pinjula.com with the subject "Delete my account". You do not need to reinstall the app. Send it from the email address of the account you signed in with, or write that address in the message. If you used Apple's "Hide My Email", give the relay address Apple created for this app — your Apple Account settings list it under Sign in with Apple.

To be sure the request comes from you, we reply to that address and delete the account once you confirm. We complete the request within 30 days at the latest.

What is deleted, and when

At once, when the deletion goes through:

  • the app's data on the phone you delete from: the pantry, shopping list, calendar, cookbook, favourites, your ratings and notes, the people you cook for and everything entered for them, badges, reminders, widgets, the usage-statistics IDs, the downloaded dish pictures and the copies the photo picker left in the app's cache. This is data that was only ever on that phone; we never had it.

At once, on our servers:

  • your account record, your subscription record and its notice rows (the small records we keep to send you subscription emails; none of them holds your email address);
  • the deletion of your sign-in is queued.

Normally within fifteen minutes:

  • your sign-in at our authentication provider, Supabase — the email address, name, profile-picture address and account identifier Google or Apple shared, and your sessions;
  • the record held under your account's ID by RevenueCat, Inc., which checks Premium purchases for us. We ask RevenueCat to delete it, and ask again about an hour, a day, three days and seven days later, in case a phone still running the app re-created it.

If any step fails, we retry until it succeeds; the request stays on file as an internal account number so that the deletion can be finished. If you signed in with Apple and delete in the app on an iPhone, Apple asks you to confirm once more, and once your account is deleted we use Apple's one-time code to ask Apple to revoke the app's Sign in with Apple tokens — only if the code belongs to the Apple Account your Pinjula account uses — which removes Pinjula from your Apple Account. Cancelling that prompt does not stop the deletion; see "Apple and Google" for when the app stays connected.

What is kept, and why

  • A minimal purchase record, for three years from each charge. For each charge: the store and its transaction number, the product, the day of the charge, the price and currency, and — when you bought from Pinjula's Premium screen — that screen's version and language and the day you pressed its buy button. It holds no name, no email address, no account ID and no sign-in. We keep it so that we can show you agreed to a subscription (California's automatic-renewal law asks for proof of consent for three years).
  • Dish names. A dish name you asked us to write a recipe for was counted with no link to your account or your phone. The count keeps the date and time each name was first and last asked for.
  • Reports about a recipe. A problem you reported with a recipe was stored with no link to your account or your phone, so deleting your account cannot find it. It is deleted 12 months after you sent it — or sooner if you write to us with enough to find it (the recipe, the day and the words of your note).
  • Usage statistics. If you allowed them, what was sent to PostHog was not linked to your account, and deleting your account cannot find it. Deleting your account deletes their IDs from the phone you delete it on.
  • Service logs. Our server's own log lines — counts and status codes, never an IP address, an account or anything you sent — and Cloudflare's record of each request to our server (time, address requested, response status, IP address and the approximate location worked out from it) are kept by Cloudflare for at most seven days. The log of sign-in events in our database keeps each event, with its IP address, account ID and email address, for 90 days, and Supabase keeps the same events, and the sign-in service's other requests, in its own log storage under its log retention for our project. They are kept to run and secure the service.
  • Google's abuse logs. Google keeps the requests made to its Gemini AI for up to 55 days to detect misuse. We cannot match them to your account, so we cannot find or delete them; they expire on their own.
  • Backups. Supabase keeps backup copies of our database, and what was deleted remains in a backup until that backup is replaced.
  • The stores' records. Apple and Google keep their own records of your purchases, under their own policies.

What stays on phones

The app's data is deleted from the phone you delete your account from, with three exceptions: the small record that keeps the app's occasional Premium suggestions rare is trimmed (its calendar days and last cooking mark go) but not deleted; the membership library keeps its own on-device cache of your plan; and on iPhone, Google's own sign-in session in the keychain can outlast uninstalling the app. Uninstalling the app deletes what it keeps in its own storage. Another phone where you used the app keeps its data until the app is uninstalled there, or until another account signs in there and the app deletes it first. A backup of your phone in your own Google or Apple account may still hold a copy of the app's data; we cannot reach it, and you can delete it in your phone's backup settings.

Apple and Google

Deleting your account removes it from our service. If you signed in with Apple and delete in the app on an iPhone, we also ask Apple to revoke the app's Sign in with Apple tokens, which removes this app from the apps connected to your Apple Account (see above). Unless that revocation goes through, the app stays connected to your Apple Account until you remove it yourself, under Sign in with Apple in your Apple Account settings. It does not go through when you delete on Android or by email; when you cancel Apple's prompt or do not answer it within a minute; when the phone is signed in to a different Apple Account from the one your Pinjula account uses — nothing is revoked then, and your Pinjula account's Apple Account stays connected; when a deletion interrupted on the phone is finished the next time the app starts; or when our server cannot make the request or Apple does not complete it. When you delete in the app, it disconnects from Google's sign-in library. That removes Pinjula from your Google Account's third-party connections on iPhone; on Android it does so only for an account signed in during the same session of the app. Otherwise, and when you delete by email, Pinjula may stay in those connections until you remove it yourself, under third-party connections in your Google Account's security settings.

A subscription is not cancelled by deleting your account. If you subscribed to Pinjula Premium, cancel it in your App Store or Google Play subscriptions; until you do, the store keeps renewing it.

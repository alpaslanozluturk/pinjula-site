What can you make for dinner with what you already have?

Take a quick inventory, choose a cooking method that suits it (a pan of pasta, a pot of soup, a frittata, a rice or grain bowl), and let one starch, one protein and one vegetable decide the dish. Most recipes survive one or two missing ingredients if you swap for the same job: acid for acid, fat for fat, a crunchy topping for a crunchy topping.

Step 1: Take stock in two minutes

Open the fridge, freezer and cupboard and write down only four things:

  • Something that fills the plate: pasta, rice, potatoes, bread, tortillas, noodles, couscous.
  • Something with protein: eggs, canned beans or fish, tofu, frozen chicken, lentils, cheese.
  • Something green or colorful: frozen vegetables, an onion and a carrot, canned tomatoes.
  • Something that adds flavor: garlic, spices, a jar of sauce, vinegar, a lemon, soy sauce.

If you cannot find all four, you still have a dinner. You have a shopping list of one item.

Step 2: Pick a method, not a recipe

Pantry cooking is easier when you choose a method first:

  • Pan pasta or noodles. Cook the starch, then toss it with whatever sautéed vegetables and a sauce or oil you have.
  • Frittata or omelette. Eggs take almost any vegetables, cheese or leftover cooked grains.
  • Soup or stew. Onion, canned tomatoes or stock, beans or lentils, and any vegetables that need using.
  • Rice or grain bowl. Cooked grain, a protein, vegetables, a sauce.
  • Quesadillas, tacos or flatbread. A tortilla and a filling is a flexible template.

Step 3: Substitute by job

When a recipe asks for something you do not have, ask what the ingredient does:

  • Lemon juice and vinegar both add acid.
  • Butter and oil both carry fat and heat.
  • A different canned bean works for the one named.
  • Fresh herbs can often be replaced by a smaller amount of dried ones, added earlier.

If the ingredient is the point of the dish (the fish in a fish stew, the eggs in a frittata), do not substitute; choose another dish.

Step 4: Let missing items become a short list

Write the one or two things that would have made tonight better and put them on the shopping list for later. Tick them off when you shop, and what you buy goes back into the pantry for next time. A list built this way stays short, which keeps trips fast.

Keep a basic stock

A few staples make the method work on nearly any night: pasta, rice, canned tomatoes, canned beans, eggs, onions, oil, a vinegar, a few spices, stock. Replace them when they run low rather than all at once.

FAQ

What can I make for dinner with just pantry staples?

Pasta with a simple sauce, a frittata, bean soup, a rice bowl or quesadillas are the usual answers. Choose the method first, then fill it with what you have.

How do I cook without a recipe?

Pick a method such as pan pasta, soup or a grain bowl. Combine a starch, a protein, a vegetable and a flavor, and taste as you go. Cooked-through temperatures still matter for meat, poultry and eggs.

What can I use if I am missing an ingredient?

Replace it by function: acid for acid (lemon for vinegar), fat for fat (oil for butter), and similar canned beans for each other. If the missing ingredient is the main point of the dish, pick another dish.

Can an app suggest dinners from my pantry?

Yes. Pinjula's pantry-only mode leaves out recipes that need something you do not have, and its shopping list holds what is missing.
